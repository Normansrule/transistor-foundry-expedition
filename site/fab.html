          Process theatres · Areas 01–03, 05–11, 13

          Pull, clean, grow, carve, dope, coat, polish, free, wire and test

          Thirteen more steps, each run by its own model. A crystal pulled from the melt takes in only part of its dopant, a clean decides how many particles and metal atoms survive to kill dies, oxide grows by the Deal–Grove law, ions stop where their energy-loss model says, dopants soak in from a surface source and spread in a second bake, an anneal heals implant damage on a thermal budget, narrow openings etch slowly because etchant struggles to reach their floor, films thin out down a trench, the polisher flattens sparse regions before dense ones, a narrow copper line loses conductance to its barrier and to surface scattering, wafer probe decides which dies are worth packaging, a selective etch frees nanosheets without thinning them too much, and Hoerni’s 1959 planar junction shows why its curved edge breaks down first. Drag the settings and every shape is recomputed.

                    Gate to silicon

                    This is a NAND. This is what it looks like in silicon.

                    Pick a logic gate and a process node. The same gate is shown six ways: the logic symbol and truth table, the transistor schematic, the mask layout a designer draws with its key dimensions, a 3D view of the cell you can pull apart layer by layer, and a slice through the finished chip. The full adder and the SRAM cell are also tiled into a 4-bit adder and a small memory array. Toggle the inputs and watch which transistors turn on.

                    Gate
                    Process
                    Inputs

                    Layouts use Mead–Conway λ rules at λ = 90 nm for the planar cell , transistor widths from logical effort , and the gridded FinFET and nanosheet pitches of the node table: 51 nm gate pitch and 28 nm metal pitch for TSMC N5 , and estimated values for the 2 nm class (for comparison, IBM’s first stacked-nanosheet devices ran at 44/48 nm gate pitch and 12 nm gate length ). Finger order is an Euler path through both networks . The full adder is the mirror adder, and wide adders replace its rippling carry with look-ahead ; the SRAM cell’s pull-downs are twice as wide as its access transistors so a read cannot flip it . Cells are teaching drawings: real libraries add taps, fill and many more design rules.

                    Virtual Fab · computed cross-section

                    Write your own traveler

                    Add process steps in the order you think a fab would run them. Each step is checked against the steps it needs; a HOLD stamp means something is missing before it. Each step that passes runs the model behind its process theatre, at the theatre's default settings or at the settings you chose in the theatres above, and the cross-section below is computed from the results: oxide grown by Deal–Grove, junctions from the implant, diffusion and anneal models, metal polished by Preston's law.
