MANUAL 10 · AREA 10 · 6 OPERATIONS

Wire the chip in copper

Dual damascene: insulator, trench, barrier, seed, plate, polish, measure

Copper cannot be etched cleanly like aluminium, so it is wired the other way round. Grooves are cut into the insulator first, lined, filled with copper and polished flat, leaving copper only in the grooves, like an inlaid damascene sword. A modern chip repeats this for a dozen or more layers.

The wiring follows the dual-damascene scheme1 and the first full copper process2; line resistivity follows the size-effect models used by the Fab Journey’s copper theatre3 with mean free paths from Gall4; polishing follows the pattern-density model5.

OP 10.01

Lay down the insulator

Deposit the insulating layer that the wires will sit in, often a low-k film that lowers the capacitance between neighbouring lines.

  • a lower dielectric constant cuts the RC delay of every wire6

Check Thickness and dielectric constant on monitors are on target.

PurposeMake the layer the wires are inlaid into
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.

Porous low-k films are fragile, and etching or stripping resist can raise their dielectric constant again, so the later steps are kept gentle6.

OP 10.02

Pattern and etch the trenches and vias

Print the via holes and the line trenches with lithography and etch both into the insulator, so one fill later makes the vias and the lines together.

  • vias and lines filled in one step: dual damascene1

Check Trench width and depth are on target.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolScanner and plasma etcher
PurposeCut the shape of the wiring into the insulator
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlTrench width (CD) and depth
Out of controlTrench too wide or too deep: rework before any metal goes in

Process engineer

The ratio of depth to width, the aspect ratio, decides how hard the next steps are: the barrier and seed must reach the bottom corners, and the plating must fill without a seam1.

OP 10.03

Line it with a barrier and a seed

Coat every surface with a thin barrier that copper cannot cross, then a thin copper seed for the plating to grow from.

  • copper must never touch silicon or oxide: the liner keeps it in2
  • the barrier conducts poorly, so it is dead area in a narrow line4

Check The barrier and seed are continuous down to the trench floor.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolPVD or ALD barrier and seed chamber
PurposeSeal the copper in and give plating a start
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSheet resistance of the seed; coverage on test trenches
Out of controlGaps at the bottom corners: voids later in the plated line

Process engineer

Copper diffuses quickly into silicon dioxide and silicon, where it ruins transistors, so the barrier wraps every line. The barrier must be thin, because in a 20 nm line every nanometre of it takes conductor away [@edelstein1997; @gall2016].

OP 10.04

Fill by electroplating

Trained staff run the plating cell. Copper grows from the seed until every trench is full, and a little beyond.

  • the plating bath is institution-only
  • additives make the trench fill from the bottom up

Check Every trench is filled with no seam; the overburden covers the wafer.

Operation specification · Tier 3 · institution-only
ToolElectroplating cell, staff only
PurposeFill the trenches with copper
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSet by the lab
Out of controlSeam or void in the fill: the lab adjusts the bath

Process engineer

Additives in the bath slow growth at the trench mouth and speed it at the floor, so the trench fills from the bottom up without a seam2. The bath chemistry is process know-how and stays with the lab; nothing about it is given here.

5

OP 10.05

Polish back to the insulator

Polish away the copper and barrier on top until only the copper in the trenches remains.

  • removal rate rises with pressure and speed: Preston’s law7
  • wide lines dish and dense arrays erode5

Check The field is clear of metal and no lines are shorted.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolChemical-mechanical polisher
PurposeLeave copper only where the wiring belongs
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlEndpoint from friction or reflected light; dishing and erosion on test structures
Out of controlResidual copper between lines: polish more; dished lines: tune the pad and slurry

Process engineer

Copper is softer than the insulator, so wide lines are polished below the surface (dishing) and dense regions lose insulator too (erosion). Both thin the line and raise its resistance5.

OP 10.06

Measure the wires

Probe test structures: long serpentines for resistance, interleaved combs for shorts.

  • a 10 nm copper line conducts about three times worse than bulk would suggest: surfaces and grain boundaries scatter electrons3

Check Line resistance and comb leakage within limits.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolParametric tester
PurposeConfirm the wiring conducts and is isolated
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlResistance per micrometre; comb leakage
Out of controlResistance high: thin line, dishing or voids; leakage: copper residue

Process engineer

Narrow lines scatter electrons at their surfaces and grain boundaries, so resistivity rises well above the bulk value just as the cross-section shrinks. That is why ruthenium and other metals with short mean free paths are being considered for the tightest layers4.

Why it changed

From aluminium to copper

Copper conducts better and resists electromigration, but it needed a whole new way of patterning.

  • Bulk copper resistivity is about 1.7 µΩ·cm, aluminium about 2.654
  • Dual damascene fills vias and lines in one step1
  • IBM announced full copper wiring in production CMOS in 19972
  • Thin films conduct worse because electrons scatter at grain boundaries and surfaces3

Institution-only Plating baths, the acids around them and polishing slurries are handled only by trained staff, and copper must be kept out of every tool that touches bare silicon.

What the models leave out

  • The cross-section shows one trench; dual damascene cuts a via and a line in one opening.
  • Resistivity follows the site’s size-effect model; real lines also depend on grain size after the anneal and on the barrier material.
  • No plating chemistry, current or polishing slurry is given: those are institution-only.
  • Electromigration and stress voiding are covered by the electromigration simulator, not this manual.
