MANUAL 7B · AREA 07 · 6 OPERATIONS

Coat every surface evenly

Thin-film deposition: chemical vapour deposition (CVD) and atomic layer deposition (ALD)

Not every film can be grown from the silicon itself. Chemical vapour deposition (CVD) lets gas molecules react on the hot wafer and leave a solid film behind. Atomic layer deposition (ALD) does the same one molecular layer at a time, so the film covers the floor and walls of a deep trench almost as thickly as the top. The precursor gases are institution-only, so those parts stay conceptual.

Step coverage follows the trench model in the Fab Journey’s deposition theatre, built on the CVD treatment in Plummer, Deal and Griffin1; ALD growth per cycle and self-limiting chemistry follow George’s review2; the high-k gate dielectric follows Intel’s 45 nm process3.

OP 7B.01

Load and pump down

Place the wafer in the chamber through a load lock and pump it down to low pressure, then bring it to the deposition temperature.

  • cluster tools pass wafers between chambers without air1

Check Base pressure and wafer temperature are at the recipe values.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCVD or ALD chamber with load lock
PurposeStart from a clean, heated, evacuated wafer
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlBase pressure, leak-up rate, wafer temperature
Out of controlLeak-up too fast: the chamber is checked before any wafer runs

Process engineer

At low pressure molecules travel in straight lines between wall hits rather than colliding with each other. That is what lets them reach the bottom of a trench, and it is the regime the deposition theatre assumes1.

OP 7B.02

Let in the precursors

Trained staff run the gas cabinet. The tool meters a precursor vapour into the chamber, where it meets the hot wafer.

  • precursor gases are institution-only
  • the tool, not the user, handles every gas line

Check Flows are steady at the recipe values set by the lab.

Operation specification · Tier 3 · institution-only
ToolGas cabinet and mass-flow controllers, staff only
PurposeBring the reacting molecules to the wafer
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSet by the lab
Out of controlFlow alarm: the tool stops and staff check the line

Process engineer

Many CVD and ALD precursors are toxic, flammable or ignite in air, so they are stored in ventilated cabinets with leak detection and handled only by trained staff1. Nothing about their chemistry or settings is given here.

3

OP 7B.03

Grow the film by CVD

Let the precursor react on the hot surfaces. The film grows on the top, the walls and the floor of every trench at once, fastest where the gas arrives first.

  • a molecule that reacts on its first hit coats only the mouth1
  • a low sticking probability lets molecules bounce down to the floor1

Check Thickness on a monitor wafer matches the target.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCVD chamber (low-pressure or plasma-enhanced)
PurposeCover the wafer with an insulator or liner
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlDeposition time against rate on monitors; temperature and pressure
Out of controlFloor too thin: lower the sticking probability or use ALD

Process engineer

n″(z) = (3s / 2w²) · n(z)

Inside a trench of width w, the gas concentration n falls with depth because every wall hit can consume it. The balance n″ = (3s/2w²)·n makes the film thin out over a depth of about w/√s, where s is the sticking probability. The Fab Journey’s deposition theatre solves that balance as the trench narrows.

OP 7B.04

Grow it one layer at a time (ALD)

Pulse the first precursor until it has covered every surface, purge, pulse the second to react with that layer, purge again. Each cycle adds one thin layer everywhere, top to floor.

  • each half-reaction stops by itself once the surface is covered2
  • aluminium oxide grows about 0.1 nm per cycle2

Check Growth per cycle on a monitor wafer is at its expected value.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolALD chamber
PurposeMake a film of exact thickness that follows every surface
Growth per cycle, aluminium oxide≈ 0.1 nm2
ControlCycle count; pulse and purge times long enough to saturate
Out of controlGrowth per cycle too high: purges too short, so the gases met and did CVD

Process engineer

thickness = cycles × growth per cycle

Because each half-cycle saturates, ALD does not depend on how much gas reaches each spot, only on whether enough does. That self-limiting step makes it conformal in deep trenches and precise to a fraction of a nanometre, at the price of being slow2.

5

OP 7B.05

Build a high-k gate dielectric

On a gate stack, use ALD to lay down a hafnium-based insulator a few nanometres thick, then cap it with the gate metal.

  • high-k metal gates entered volume production at 45 nm in 20073
  • high-k gate dielectrics were the first big ALD product2

Check Thickness and capacitance on monitors are on target.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolALD chamber in a gate-stack cluster
PurposeThin the equivalent oxide without the leakage
First volume high-k metal gate node45 nm (2007)3
ControlPhysical thickness, capacitance-equivalent thickness, leakage
Out of controlLeakage high: the film is too thin or has pinholes

Process engineer

A hafnium-based insulator has a much higher dielectric constant than silicon dioxide, so it can be physically thicker for the same capacitance, which cuts tunnelling leakage3. Only ALD could make it thin and even enough2.

6

OP 7B.06

Measure thickness and coverage

Measure the blanket thickness by ellipsometry, then cut a test trench and compare the film on the floor and walls with the top.

  • step coverage = thinnest wall or floor ÷ top thickness1

Check Step coverage meets the spec for this layer.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolEllipsometer; cross-section electron microscope
PurposeConfirm the film is thick enough everywhere
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlBlanket thickness map; step coverage on test trenches
Out of controlKeyhole void or thin floor: change method, taper the trench or etch back and redeposit

Process engineer

If the mouth closes before the floor fills, a void is sealed inside: a keyhole. Voids raise resistance and fail early. Low-sticking CVD and ALD fill from the walls inward and stay seamless1.

Why it changed

From sputtering to CVD to ALD

Each step down in trench width needed a method whose molecules reach further before they stick.

  • Atomic layer epitaxy began in the 1970s for thin-film displays2
  • ALD aluminium oxide grows about 0.1 nm per cycle2
  • Hafnium-based high-k gate dielectrics entered volume CMOS at 45 nm in 20073
  • A low sticking probability gives better step coverage in CVD1

Institution-only CVD and ALD precursors are toxic, flammable or ignite in air, and the tools run hot and under vacuum. Only trained staff handle the gases.

What the models leave out

  • Sticking probability is one number per process; real precursors change it with temperature and surface.
  • The trench model works in molecular flow, ignoring gas-phase reactions and the boundary layer above the wafer.
  • No precursor, temperature or flow is given: those are institution-only.
  • Film stress, density, impurities and the interface layer under a high-k film are described, not modelled.
