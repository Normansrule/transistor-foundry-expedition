MANUAL 1 · AREA 01 · 6 OPERATIONS

Grow the crystal and cut the wafer

Czochralski growth: melt, seed, neck, body, grind, slice, polish, sort

Every chip starts as one perfect crystal. Ultra-pure silicon is melted with a pinch of dopant, a seed crystal is dipped in and slowly pulled up, and the melt freezes onto it atom by atom in the seed’s own arrangement. The ingot is then ground, sliced into wafers and polished to a mirror. The dopant does not freeze in evenly, which is why resistivity changes along the ingot.

Dopant along the ingot follows the Scheil equation1 with segregation coefficients from Trumbore2 and the effective-k theory of Burton, Prim and Slichter3, as in the Fab Journey’s crystal theatre; crystal pulling follows Czochralski4, Teal and Little5 and Dash6; wafer practice follows Plummer, Deal and Griffin7.

OP 1.01

Melt the silicon and add the dopant

Load polysilicon into a quartz crucible, add the dopant and melt it all under argon. The melt is doped richer than the target, because the crystal will take in only a fraction k of it.

  • the crystal takes in only a fraction k of the dopant3
  • boron k ≈ 0.8, phosphorus k ≈ 0.352

Check Melt temperature steady; dopant weight logged.

OP 1.02

Dip the seed and pull a thin neck

Lower a small seed crystal of the chosen orientation into the melt, let it wet, then pull a thin neck before widening.

  • a thin neck lets dislocations grow out: dislocation-free silicon6
  • the seed sets the orientation of the whole ingot, (100) for MOS7

Check The neck is thin and even; the meniscus is stable.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCrystal puller
PurposeStart one perfect crystal
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSeed rotation, pull speed, neck diameter
Out of controlDislocations in the neck: melt back and dip again

Process engineer

The thermal shock of dipping makes dislocations in the seed. Pulling a thin, fast neck lets them run out to the surface and vanish, so the ingot above it grows free of them, as Dash showed in 19596.

3

OP 1.03

Pull the body

Widen to the full diameter, then pull the crystal slowly upward while seed and crucible turn in opposite directions.

  • a slower pull or a cooler melt makes the crystal wider7
  • the dopant piles up in the melt, so resistivity falls along the ingot1

Check Diameter on target along the whole body.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCrystal puller with diameter control
PurposeGrow the full-diameter crystal
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlPull rate, rotation, melt temperature, diameter camera
Out of controlDiameter swing: the controller adjusts pull rate and heater power

Process engineer

C_s(f) = k·C₀·(1 − f)^(k−1)

As the crystal grows, the interface rejects a fraction 1 − k of the dopant back into the shrinking melt, so the melt and the next slice of crystal get richer. The solid follows C_s = kC₀(1 − f)^(k−1), where f is the fraction already frozen1. Stirring and pull rate shift the effective k3.

OP 1.04

Grind and mark the orientation

Grind the ingot to an exact diameter and cut a notch or flat along it that marks the crystal orientation.

  • the notch tells every later tool which way the crystal faces7

Check Diameter and notch position within tolerance.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolCylindrical grinder; X-ray orientation
PurposeMake every wafer the same size and orientation
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlDiameter; notch angle by X-ray
Out of controlNotch misplaced: regrind or downgrade

Process engineer

Lithography and etching depend on the crystal planes, so every wafer carries its orientation with it. X-ray diffraction finds the planes before the notch is ground7.

OP 1.05

Slice, flatten and polish

Saw the ingot into thin wafers, remove the sawing damage and polish one face to a mirror.

  • the damage-removal etch is institution-only chemistry
  • the final polish is chemical-mechanical, like planarization7

Check Thickness, flatness and surface meet the spec.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolWire saw, lapper, polisher
PurposeTurn the ingot into flat, mirror-smooth wafers
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlThickness, total thickness variation, roughness
Out of controlScratches or haze: repolish; warp: reject

Process engineer

Sawing leaves a damaged layer that would crack and trap impurities, so it is lapped and chemically etched away before the polish. The etch chemistry stays with the wafer maker7.

OP 1.06

Measure and sort

Measure resistivity at points along the ingot and on each wafer. Wafers inside the customer’s band are prime; the rest go to looser uses.

  • resistivity falls from seed to tail for any k below 11
  • resistivity comes from doping through the mobility8

Check Resistivity within the band; no dislocations or particles.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolFour-point probe; particle scanner
PurposeKeep only wafers that meet the spec
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlResistivity map; particle count
Out of controlOut of band: sold to a looser spec or recycled

Process engineer

ρ = 1 / (q·μ(N)·N)

Only part of each ingot stays inside a tight resistivity band; the crystal theatre shows how much for each dopant and tolerance. Resistivity follows ρ = 1/(qμN) with the mobility falling as doping rises8.

Where it came from

From a metals experiment to 300 mm wafers

A method for measuring how fast metals crystallise became the start of every chip.

  • Czochralski pulled metal crystals from a melt in 1918 to measure crystallisation speed4
  • Teal and Little pulled single crystals of germanium at Bell Labs in 19505
  • Dash’s thin neck gave dislocation-free silicon in 19596
  • Boron’s segregation coefficient is about 0.8, phosphorus’s about 0.352

Institution-only Crystal pullers hold molten silicon above 1400 °C under argon and are run only by trained staff; the damage-removal etch uses institution-only chemistry.

What the models leave out

  • Dopant follows the Scheil equation with one effective k; real pulls vary it with pull rate and stirring.
  • Oxygen from the quartz crucible, which every Czochralski wafer contains, is described, not modelled.
  • Float-zone growth and silicon-on-insulator wafers are left out.
  • No etch chemistry or furnace setting is given: the puller and the etch are staff-run.
