MANUAL 6A · AREA 06 · 6 OPERATIONS

Bake the dopant into the silicon

Furnace diffusion: open windows, predeposit, remove the source, drive in, measure

Before ion implanters, every junction was made in a furnace, and teaching cleanrooms still make them that way. A dopant-rich source sits on bare silicon, atoms soak in from the surface, the source is removed and a second bake drives them deeper. Oxide decides where the dopant can go. The dopant sources are institution-only, so those stages stay conceptual.

Profiles follow the textbook predeposition and drive-in solutions1 with intrinsic diffusivities2; oxide masking follows Frosch and Derick3; run values come from a four-mask NMOS run in a university teaching cleanroom4,5.

OP 6A.01

Check the windows are open

Look at the wafer under the microscope. Where the oxide has been etched away, bare silicon shows; everywhere else oxide will block the dopant.

  • oxide masks dopant diffusion3
  • in MOS the gate masks the channel

Check Windows are clean and fully open; no oxide film left in them.

Process engineer

Dopants diffuse far more slowly in silicon dioxide than in silicon, so an oxide layer masks the silicon beneath it3. Hoerni’s planar process made every junction this way, through windows in the oxide6.

OP 6A.02

Coat the dopant source

A thin film rich in dopant is put on the wafer by trained staff. It touches the silicon only where the windows are open.

  • the source keeps the surface concentration fixed
  • dopant sources are institution-only

Check The film covers the wafer evenly.

The source can be a spun-on film, a solid source wafer or a dopant gas; all hold the silicon surface at a high, fixed concentration while the furnace runs1. Every source chemistry is institution-only, and some are highly toxic.

OP 6A.03

Predeposit in the furnace

In the hot furnace dopant atoms hop from the film into the open silicon. The silicon near the surface fills up first.

  • the surface stays pinned at the source’s concentration
  • the dose grows only as the square root of time

Check A control wafer travels with the batch.

Operation specification · Tier 3 · institution-only
ToolHorizontal tube furnace with the source, staff only
PurposeSet the dopant dose
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSet by the lab; control wafer measured afterwards
Out of controlControl wafer out of range: the lab decides on a rerun

Process engineer

C(x) = Cs · erfc(x / 2√(Dt))

With the surface pinned at Cs the profile is a complementary error function, and the dose grows as √t: double the time and only 41 % more dopant goes in1. The furnace step with a dopant source is institution-only.

OP 6A.04

Remove the source

The spent film is removed, so the amount of dopant in the silicon is now fixed.

  • from here the dose cannot change
  • institution-only chemistry

Check The control wafer’s sheet resistance confirms dopant went in.

Removing the film is a wet chemical step and stays institution-only1. Teaching labs keep a second control wafer so a weak predeposition can be repeated.

OP 6A.05

Drive in and grow oxide

A second, longer bake without any source spreads the same dopant deeper. The same bake grows an oxide over everything.

  • teaching run: 1050 °C for 37 minutes4
  • teaching run: 62.9 nm of oxide on the control4
  • junction creeps sideways about 80 % of its depth1

Check Oxide on the bare control wafer has the target thickness.

Process engineer

C(x) = Q / √(πDt) · exp(−x² / 4Dt)

With a fixed dose the profile relaxes into a half-Gaussian, and Dt adds up over both bakes1. The Virtual Fab follows this run’s drive-in and puts its computed oxide, 62.0 nm, beside the measured 62.9 nm.

OP 6A.06

Measure the sheet resistance

Touch four needles in a line to a diffused test resistor or the control wafer. Push current through the outer two and read the voltage across the inner two.

  • teaching run: 57.1 Ω across a 201 µm × 11 µm resistor5
  • that is about 3.1 Ω per square5

Check The sheet resistance is in the expected range.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolFour-point probe
PurposeCheck the doped layer conducts as designed

Process engineer

R_s = R · W / L

R_s = R·W/L. The four-point probe needs no correction for contact resistance because the voltage pins carry no current1. The site’s intrinsic-diffusivity model gives about ten times this value: heavy phosphorus diffuses faster than the intrinsic rate2.

Compare

Furnace diffusion versus ion implantation

Both put dopant into the silicon. A furnace soaks it in from the surface; an implanter fires a counted dose to a chosen depth.

  • Oxide masks dopant diffusion, the discovery that made planar windows possible3
  • Hoerni’s planar process formed junctions by diffusion through oxide windows6
  • Ion implantation took over most doping steps from the 1970s1
  • Teaching run: a diffused resistor measured about 3.1 Ω per square5

Institution-only Dopant sources, whether spun-on films, solid sources or gases, are institution-only. So is stripping the source. This manual gives only what each stage does and what was measured afterwards.

What the models leave out

  • Diffusivity is the intrinsic value; heavy doping, oxidation and point defects speed real diffusion up, so real junctions are deeper.
  • The predeposition time and surface concentration of the teaching run were not recorded.
  • Dopant segregation into the growing oxide is ignored.
  • No source chemistry or furnace procedure is given: those stages are institution-only.
