MANUAL 6B · AREA 06 · 6 OPERATIONS

Fire dopant atoms into the silicon

Ion implantation: mask, tilt, implant, see the damage, anneal, measure

An implanter turns dopant atoms into ions, speeds them up through a voltage and fires them into the wafer. The voltage sets how deep they go and a meter counts every one, so the dose and depth are far more precise than any furnace diffusion. The ion source uses institution-only gases, so that part stays conceptual.

Ranges follow the stopping-power model of the site’s implant simulator, checked against published examples1; implanter practice and annealing follow Plummer, Deal and Griffin2; Dennard’s scaled MOSFET shows why implantation took over3.

OP 6B.01

Check the mask is thick enough

Measure the resist. It must stop every ion where the silicon should stay undoped, and its edges decide where the doped region starts.

  • gates and spacers mask too: self-alignment2

Check Resist thickness is well above the ion range for the planned energy.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolThickness gauge
PurposeMake sure the mask will stop the ions
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlResist thickness against ion range
Out of controlResist too thin: ions punch through; recoat

Process engineer

High-energy implants need microns of resist. In a self-aligned MOS process the polysilicon gate itself masks the channel, so source and drain land exactly beside it2.

OP 6B.02

Make the ion beam

Trained staff run the ion source, which ionises a dopant-bearing feed. A magnet then picks out only the ions of the right mass.

  • feed gases are institution-only
  • the analysing magnet sorts ions by mass

Check The beam current is steady on the right mass.

Operation specification · Tier 3 · institution-only
ToolIon source and analysing magnet, staff only
PurposeMake a pure beam of one dopant
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSet by the lab
Out of controlWrong mass peak: the lab retunes the magnet

Process engineer

Many dopant feed gases are highly toxic and are handled only inside the tool by trained staff2. Nothing about them is given here.

3

OP 6B.03

Implant at a tilt

Accelerate the ions and scan the beam across the tilted wafer until the meter has counted the full dose.

  • energy sets depth: phosphorus at 100 keV stops near 0.12 µm1
  • boron at 80 keV stops near 0.24 µm1
  • a few degrees of tilt stops channelling2

Check The integrated dose matches the recipe.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolIon implanter
PurposePut a counted dose at a chosen depth
P at 100 keV, projected range≈ 0.12 µm1
B at 80 keV, projected range≈ 0.24 µm1
ControlBeam current integrated over time; scan uniformity
Out of controlDose off: the run is held and the Faraday cup checked

Process engineer

N(x) = Q / (√(2π) ΔRp) · exp(−(x − Rp)² / 2ΔRp²)

Each ion loses energy to nuclei and to electrons until it stops. The stopping depths form a near-Gaussian with projected range Rp and straggle ΔRp; the dose is the beam charge divided by the ion charge and the area2.

OP 6B.04

See the damage

Look at what the ions left behind: every one displaced many silicon atoms, and a heavy dose leaves the top layer amorphous. Most dopant is not yet active.

  • one ion displaces hundreds of atoms2

Check A damage or sheet-resistance check on a monitor wafer.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolOptical damage monitor
PurposeKnow the state before the anneal
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlDamage signal against dose
Out of controlUnexpected damage level: check dose and beam heating

Process engineer

Implanted atoms sit mostly between lattice sites at first. Pre-amorphising implants are sometimes used on purpose so the crystal regrows cleanly from the undamaged silicon below2.

5

OP 6B.05

Strip the resist and anneal

Remove the resist, then heat the wafer briefly. The crystal repairs itself and the dopant moves onto lattice sites, where it conducts.

  • spike and millisecond anneals activate with little diffusion2

Check Sheet resistance falls to its target.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolRapid thermal processor
PurposeActivate the dopant and heal the crystal
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlPeak temperature and time
Out of controlSheet resistance high: too little activation; junction deep: too much heat

Process engineer

Annealing also lets the dopant diffuse, widening the profile by about √(2Dt). Keeping junctions shallow while activating fully is one of the hardest trade-offs in a short-channel transistor2.

OP 6B.06

Measure the result

Map the sheet resistance across a monitor wafer with a four-point probe. Less often, a lab sputters a crater and counts the dopant depth by depth.

  • sheet resistance catches dose errors within minutes2

Check Sheet resistance mean and spread within limits.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolFour-point probe; SIMS for profiles
PurposeConfirm dose and activation
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSheet resistance map
Out of controlNon-uniform map: check the beam scan

Process engineer

Secondary-ion mass spectrometry gives the real profile, including the channelling tail and the diffusion during the anneal, against which the simulator’s Gaussian can be checked2.

Why it won

From furnace diffusion to ion implantation

Implantation sets dose and depth separately and precisely, which scaled transistors needed.

  • Dennard’s 1974 scaled MOSFET was designed around ion-implanted doping3
  • Implanted channel and source/drain doping replaced furnace diffusion in MOS during the 1970s2
  • Phosphorus at 100 keV stops near 0.12 µm in silicon1
  • Boron at 80 keV stops near 0.24 µm1

Institution-only Implanter ion sources use dopant feed gases that are institution-only, and implanters work at high voltage. Only trained staff run them.

What the models leave out

  • The profile is a Gaussian; real profiles are skewed and have a channelling tail.
  • Damage, amorphisation and transient enhanced diffusion during the anneal are described, not modelled here.
  • No feed gas, source setting or implanter procedure is given: those are institution-only.
  • Lateral straggle under the mask edge is taken equal to the vertical straggle.
