Make a transistor on glass

An oxide thin-film transistor (IGZO): another process family, built at low temperature with no crystal at all

Not every transistor starts from a silicon crystal. The screen you are reading is driven by thin-film transistors built on glass, layer by deposited layer. Amorphous oxide semiconductors such as indium–gallium–zinc oxide (IGZO) can even be made at room temperature on plastic. This manual builds a bottom-gate oxide transistor with the operations from the earlier manuals.

The device follows the thin-film transistor of Weimer1 and the room-temperature amorphous-oxide transistor of Nomura et al.2; the flexible processor example is Ozer et al.3; deposition and patterning follow Manuals 4A, 5 and 7B and Plummer, Deal and Griffin4.

OP 12.01

Pattern a metal gate on the glass

Deposit a metal film on the glass and pattern it into gate lines. The gate sits at the bottom of this transistor.

  • the first thin-film transistor was shown in 19621

Check Gate lines continuous; edges smooth.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolSputter, aligner, etch
PurposeMake the gate first, on an insulating sheet
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlLine width; sheet resistance
Out of controlRough edges: they will thin the insulator above them

Process engineer

With no crystal to build on, every layer is deposited. Putting the gate first lets the semiconductor go on last, over a smooth insulator1.

OP 12.02

Deposit the gate insulator

Cover everything with a thin insulating film by CVD or sputtering.

  • deposited, not grown: there is no silicon to oxidise4

Check Thickness and leakage on target.

Process engineer

On silicon the best insulator is grown from the wafer itself. On glass it has to be deposited, so its quality sets much of the transistor’s behaviour4.

OP 12.03

Sputter the oxide semiconductor

Sputter a thin amorphous IGZO film over the gate and pattern it into islands. It works without being crystallised, and it can be made at room temperature.

  • amorphous oxide transistors made at room temperature on plastic2

Check Island thickness and resistance on monitors on target.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolSputter chamber
PurposeLay the channel over the gate
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlFilm thickness; oxygen content set by the lab
Out of controlChannel too conductive: the transistor will not switch off

Process engineer

Electrons in these oxides travel through overlapping metal orbitals, which work even when the atoms are disordered, so the film needs no crystal and no high temperature2.

OP 12.04

Add source and drain

Deposit and pattern metal over the ends of each island, leaving a gap over the gate: that gap is the channel length.

  • the gap between source and drain is the channel length1

Check Gap length on target; contacts ohmic.

OP 12.05

Seal and anneal

Cover the transistor with a protective film and give it a mild bake, which steadies the oxide semiconductor.

  • sealing keeps moisture and oxygen from shifting the threshold2

Check Threshold stable under bias stress.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCVD chamber; oven
PurposeProtect and stabilise the transistor
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlThreshold shift under bias
Out of controlThreshold drifts: improve the seal or the bake

Process engineer

Oxide semiconductors respond to the gases around them, so an unsealed transistor drifts. The bake stays far below what a silicon furnace uses, which is why plastic sheets survive it2.

OP 12.06

Measure and compare

Probe the transfer curve: drain current against gate voltage. Compare its on–off ratio and threshold with the silicon transistors in the other manuals.

  • a bendable RISC-V processor was built from 0.6 µm IGZO transistors3

Check On–off ratio and threshold within spec across the sheet.

Another family

No crystal, no furnace

Thin-film transistors trade speed for building on glass and plastic.

  • Weimer showed the thin-film transistor in 19621
  • Amorphous oxide transistors were made at room temperature on plastic in 20042
  • A bendable RISC-V processor used 0.6 µm IGZO transistors in 20243
  • Every layer is deposited, because there is no silicon to grow oxide from4

Institution-only Sputter and CVD tools run under vacuum and some CVD gases are institution-only; the etches are staff-run.

What the models leave out

  • The cross-section is schematic; real films are tens of nanometres thick.
  • No sputter target composition, gas mix or etch is given: those are set by the lab.
  • Bias-stress drift, light sensitivity and contact resistance are described, not modelled.
  • Top-gate and self-aligned oxide transistors, and low-temperature polysilicon, are left out.
