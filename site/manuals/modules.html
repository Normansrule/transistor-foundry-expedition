MANUAL 9 · AREA 09 · 6 OPERATIONS

Build the transistor in three dimensions

Device modules: isolation, high-k metal gate, fins, a silicon–germanium stack, release and gate wrap

A modern transistor is not a flat strip any more. To keep the gate in control of a channel only a few nanometres long, the channel was stood on edge as a fin and then split into stacked sheets that the gate wraps on every side. This manual walks through the modules that build it, each made of the operations in the earlier manuals.

The module sequence follows the multigate review by Ferain, Colinge and Colinge1, the DELTA fin2, Intel’s 45 nm high-k metal gate3 and 14 nm FinFET4, and IBM’s stacked nanosheets5; the sheet release follows the Fab Journey’s release theatre.

OP 9.01

Isolate each transistor

Etch shallow trenches around every active area, fill them with oxide and polish flat, so neighbouring transistors cannot talk to each other.

  • shallow trench isolation replaced the older grown LOCOS oxide6

Check Trenches filled with no voids; the surface is flat.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolEtcher, CVD, polisher
PurposeSeparate the transistors
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlTrench depth; fill voids; step height after polish
Out of controlVoid in the fill or dishing: rework the deposition or the polish

Process engineer

The trench is etched, lined, filled by deposition and polished back to the silicon, combining Manuals 5, 7B and 86.

OP 9.02

Put a high-k metal gate on a flat channel

Grow a thin interface oxide, deposit a high-k insulator by ALD and cap it with a metal gate. This was the planar transistor’s last big change.

  • high-k metal gates entered volume production at 45 nm in 20073

Check Gate capacitance up, leakage down, threshold on target.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolALD and metal deposition
PurposeThin the gate insulator electrically without the leakage
First volume high-k metal gate node45 nm (2007)3
ControlEquivalent oxide thickness, gate leakage, threshold voltage
Out of controlThreshold off: the gate-metal work function is wrong; leakage high: the high-k film is thin

Process engineer

A higher dielectric constant gives the same capacitance from a thicker film, so electrons can no longer tunnel straight through it. Polysilicon gates were replaced by metals because they did not work with the new insulator3.

OP 9.03

Stand the channel up as a fin

Pattern and etch narrow silicon walls, then recess the isolation oxide so the top of each wall stands free. The gate will wrap three sides.

  • the DELTA fin transistor was shown in 19892
  • 14 nm FinFETs needed self-aligned double patterning4

Check Fin width and height on target; fins straight.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolScanner with multiple patterning; etcher
PurposeGive the gate control from three sides
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlFin width, height and pitch; fin bending
Out of controlBent or thinned fins: tune the etch and the oxide recess

Process engineer

A gate on three sides holds the channel off far better than a gate on one, so leakage stays low at short lengths1. Fins narrower than the lithography can print are made by spacer patterning4.

OP 9.04

Grow a silicon–germanium stack

For nanosheets, grow alternating layers of silicon and silicon–germanium on the wafer, then etch them into fins.

  • epitaxy gases are institution-only
  • the silicon layers become the channels; the silicon–germanium is sacrificial

Check Layer thicknesses and germanium content on target, with no defects.

Operation specification · Tier 3 · institution-only
ToolEpitaxy reactor, staff only
PurposeBuild the layers that become the stacked channels
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSet by the lab
Out of controlDefects in the stack: the lab requalifies the reactor

Process engineer

Each layer grows in the crystal arrangement of the one below. The silicon–germanium is there only to hold the silicon sheets apart until the gate goes in5. The reactor gases are institution-only.

OP 9.05

Release the sheets and wrap the gate

With the source and drain in place, remove the dummy gate, etch the silicon–germanium away selectively and fill the gaps with high-k and gate metal, so the gate surrounds every sheet.

  • the release etch chemistry is institution-only
  • inner spacers protect the source and drain while the sheets are freed

Check No silicon–germanium left; every sheet fully wrapped.

Operation specification · Tier 3 · institution-only
ToolSelective etch and replacement-gate tools, staff only
PurposeLet the gate grip each channel from every side
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlSet by the lab
Out of controlLeftover silicon–germanium or thinned sheets: the lab adjusts the etch

Process engineer

The release etch removes silicon–germanium far faster than silicon, but not infinitely faster, so the sheet edges thin while the middle is still being freed. The Fab Journey’s release theatre computes that trade-off without giving any chemistry5.

OP 9.06

Measure the transistors

Probe test transistors for drive current, leakage and threshold, and cut cross-sections to check every sheet.

  • nanosheet transistors entered volume production at the 2 nm class in 20257

Check Drive current and leakage on target across the wafer.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolParametric tester; cross-section microscope
PurposeConfirm the transistors switch as designed
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlOn-current, off-current, threshold voltage, sheet thickness
Out of controlThreshold shifted: sheets thinned in the release; leakage: incomplete wrap

Process engineer

Thinner sheets raise the threshold voltage and cut the drive current, so sheet thickness measured in cross-section is tied back to the electrical results5.

Why it changed

From a flat gate to a gate on every side

Each generation gave the gate more grip on the channel so the transistor could keep shrinking.

  • High-k metal gates replaced oxide and polysilicon at 45 nm in 20073
  • The DELTA fin transistor was shown in 19892
  • Stacked nanosheets were shown as FinFET’s successor in 20175
  • Nanosheet transistors entered volume production at the 2 nm class in 20257

Institution-only Epitaxy gases and the selective release etch are institution-only; every module runs in staff-operated tools.

What the models leave out

  • The pictures are schematic: real fins and sheets are a few nanometres thick and drawn far larger here.
  • Source and drain epitaxy, inner spacers and the replacement-gate sequence are named, not drawn step by step.
  • No epitaxy gas, etch chemistry or recipe is given: those are institution-only.
  • Strain, work-function tuning and backside power delivery are left out.
