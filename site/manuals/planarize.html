MANUAL 8 · AREA 08 · 7 OPERATIONS

Make the surface flat again

Planarization: spin-on glass in older processes, chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) today

Every patterned layer leaves bumps, and each new layer copies them. After a few layers the surface is too uneven for the next lithography step to focus on. Planarization flattens it again: first with liquid spin-on glass, and since the 1990s with chemical mechanical polishing, which grinds the wafer flat with a pad and a slurry.

CMP follows Preston’s law1 and the pattern-density model the Fab Journey’s CMP theatre uses2; tool facts come from the CMP article3.

OP 8.01

See the problem

Look at the wafer after deposition. The oxide copies every line underneath, so the top is a row of bumps.

  • lithography needs the surface inside the depth of focus3

Check Measure the step height across the lines.

Process engineer

Each new film follows the shape under it. Without planarization the steps add up layer after layer, and a lens with a shallow depth of focus cannot image the top and bottom of a step at once3.

OP 8.02

Older fix: spin-on glass

Spin a liquid glass onto the wafer like resist. It flows into the low places, then a bake turns it solid.

  • spun, then cured in a furnace4
  • smooths but does not fully flatten

Check Gaps are filled and the film has no cracks.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolSpin coater and cure furnace
PurposeFill gaps and soften steps
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlThickness and crack inspection
Out of controlCracks: thinner coats or a slower cure

Process engineer

Spin-on glass fills narrow gaps and rounds steps off, but it only smooths the surface locally: wide features still leave a step. It was widely used before chemical mechanical polishing took over4.

3

OP 8.03

Mount face down on the pad

Hold the wafer upside down in a carrier and press it onto a rotating polishing pad wetted with slurry.

  • porous polymer pad, 30–50 µm pores3
  • abrasive slurry, often cerium dioxide3

Check Pad conditioned, slurry flowing, carrier pressure set.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCMP polisher: platen, pad, carrier, slurry arm, pad conditioner
PurposeSet up the polish
Pad pore size30–50 µm3
ControlPad condition; slurry flow; down-force
Out of controlGlazed pad, slow removal: recondition the pad

Process engineer

The pad is consumed as it works and is regularly reconditioned3. The slurry is both chemical and mechanical: its chemistry softens the surface and its abrasive particles wipe the softened layer away3.

OP 8.04

Polish the high spots first

Spin pad and wafer. The pad touches the bumps first, so they wear away fastest.

  • Preston: rate = Kₚ · P · V1
  • raised areas carry the pressure2

Check Removal rate on a monitor wafer matches the expected value.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCMP polisher
PurposeRemove the topography
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlRemoval rate (Preston) and time
Out of controlRate drifts: check pad and slurry

Process engineer

rate = Kₚ · P · V

Preston’s law says removal rate is proportional to pressure times sliding speed1. Over a pattern the pad rests only on the high areas at first, so their local pressure is higher and they polish faster until the step is gone2.

OP 8.05

Stop when it is flat

Keep polishing until the bumps are gone and the oxide over the lines has the target thickness.

  • brings the surface within the depth of field3

Check The surface is flat within what lithography can focus on.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCMP polisher with endpoint or timed polish
PurposeReach a flat surface at target thickness
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlRemaining thickness by reflectometry
Out of controlToo thin: the lines may be exposed; scrap or add oxide

Process engineer

On metal layers the polish is designed to stop on the interface between copper and oxide3, so the metal is left only inside the trenches: the damascene method.

6

OP 8.06

Mind the pattern density

Check dense and sparse regions. Dense patterns polish more slowly, sparse ones faster, so the die is not perfectly flat.

  • dense regions planarize more slowly2
  • fix: dummy fill in empty areas

Check Thickness difference between dense and sparse areas is within limit.

Operation specification · Tier 0 · safe
ToolFilm-thickness mapping
PurposeCatch layout-dependent unevenness
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlWithin-die thickness range
Out of controlToo uneven: add dummy fill to the layout

Process engineer

Because the pad shares its pressure over the raised area, local removal depends on the layout’s pattern density2. Designers add dummy fill so every region has a similar density. Copper lines can also be dished below the oxide, and dense arrays eroded: both are density effects.

OP 8.07

Clean off the slurry

Scrub and rinse the wafer so no slurry particles stay behind.

  • post-CMP clean4

Check Particle count after clean is within limit.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolPost-CMP brush cleaner
PurposeRemove slurry and debris
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlParticle count; scratch inspection
Out of controlScratches or particles: check the pad, slurry filter and cleaner

Process engineer

Slurry particles left on the wafer become defects in the next layer. CMP can also cause stress cracking, delamination at weak interfaces and corrosion from the slurry chemicals3.

Where CMP is used

Three polishes in every modern chip

CMP is not one step but a family, each tuned to stop on a different material.

  • Shallow trench isolation: the oxide overburden left after trench fill is polished away3
  • Tungsten contacts and plugs are polished back to the oxide3
  • Copper damascene: the polish stops at the copper–oxide interface3
  • Removal rate follows pressure × speed (Preston’s law)1
  • Pattern density sets how fast each region planarizes2

What the models leave out

  • Removal rates, pad materials and slurries vary widely; the model uses teaching values.
  • Pad wear, temperature and slurry chemistry are not modelled.
  • Spin-on glass chemistry and cure recipes are not given.
