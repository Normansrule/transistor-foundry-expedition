MANUAL 7A · AREA 07 · 7 OPERATIONS

Wire it up with aluminium

Metallization in a teaching process: open contacts, evaporate aluminium, measure, pattern, etch, sinter

Transistors are useless until they are wired together. In the planar era and in teaching cleanrooms today, a film of aluminium covers the whole wafer, touches the silicon through contact holes and is then etched into wires and pads. A low-temperature bake makes each contact conduct well.

The sequence and measurements follow a four-mask NMOS run in a university teaching cleanroom1 and its process manual2; deposition basics follow Plummer, Deal and Griffin3; the film thickness uses the bulk resistivity of aluminium4.

OP 7A.01

Check the contact holes

Under the microscope, look into each contact hole. The oxide must be gone right down to the doped silicon, or the metal will not touch it.

  • teaching run: contact holes came out 2.39 µm wider than drawn1

Check Every hole is open to silicon; widths are recorded.

ToolInspection microscope
PurposeConfirm the contacts are open
Width change after etch+2.39 µm1
ControlHole width; colour of the hole floor
Out of controlOxide left in a hole: an open contact, so etch again

Process engineer

The wet contact etch undercuts the resist, so holes grow; in the teaching run agitation in the bath made the contacts the widest features of all1.

OP 7A.02

Load and pump down

Mount the wafers face down above the source in the evaporator and pump the chamber to high vacuum.

  • vacuum lets atoms fly straight to the wafer3

Check The pressure gauge reaches the lab’s base pressure.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolThermal evaporator
PurposeClear the path for the metal atoms
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlBase pressure
Out of controlPressure will not fall: look for a leak or a dirty chamber

Process engineer

At high vacuum an evaporated atom travels further than the chamber is wide before it hits a gas molecule, so it lands in a straight line from the source3. That is also why evaporation covers steep steps poorly.

OP 7A.03

Evaporate the aluminium

Heat the source until aluminium evaporates. A thin, even film condenses over the whole wafer and into every contact hole.

  • aluminium heated in a tungsten boat in vacuum1
  • the film covers the whole wafer2

Check The thickness monitor reaches its target; the film is mirror-bright.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolThermal evaporator with a resistance-heated boat
PurposeCoat the wafer with metal
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlDeposition rate and thickness monitor
Out of controlHazy film: the vacuum or the source was dirty

Process engineer

Evaporation is the oldest way to lay down metal; sputtering, which knocks atoms off a target with ions, replaced it in industry because it covers steps better and handles alloys3. Noyce’s 1959 patent already ran evaporated aluminium leads over the oxide5.

OP 7A.04

Measure the film

Put a four-point probe on the blanket aluminium and read its sheet resistance.

  • teaching run: 0.1229 Ω per square1
  • bulk aluminium: 2.65 µΩ·cm4

Check Sheet resistance in the expected range.

ToolFour-point probe
PurposeCheck the film thickness electrically
Sheet resistance0.1229 Ω/sq1
Bulk resistivity2.65 µΩ·cm4

Out of controlToo high: a thin film; check the source charge and the monitor

Process engineer

t = ρ / R_s

Thickness follows from t = ρ/R_s. With the bulk value of 2.65 µΩ·cm4 the run’s 0.1229 Ω/sq1 means about 216 nm of aluminium; thin films conduct a little worse than bulk, so the real film is probably slightly thicker.

OP 7A.05

Pattern the wires

Coat, expose and develop resist with the metal mask, exactly as in Manual 4A. Resist now covers the wires and pads you want to keep.

  • the fourth and last mask of the teaching process1

Check Resist lines line up with the contacts underneath.

PurposeDefine the wiring
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.

Process engineer

Overlay matters more here than anywhere: a wire shifted off its contact leaves a transistor unconnected. The teaching run measured its earlier layer-to-layer shifts at up to 1.4 µm1.

OP 7A.06

Etch the aluminium

Exposed aluminium is etched away by trained staff, leaving wires and pads under the resist. The resist is then removed.

  • lines come out a little narrower than drawn
  • institution-only chemistry

Check Wires are separate: no metal bridges between neighbours.

Operation specification · Tier 3 · institution-only
ToolWet bench, staff only
PurposeTurn the film into wiring
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlLine width after etch
Out of controlBridges between lines: more etch; missing lines: less

Process engineer

A wet aluminium etch undercuts the resist like every wet etch. In the teaching run the metal lines came out 0.88 µm narrower than drawn on average1. Industry etches aluminium in a plasma instead, so the walls stay vertical3.

7

OP 7A.07

Sinter the contacts

Bake the wafer gently so the aluminium and silicon bond at every contact and the contact resistance falls.

  • teaching run: 400 °C for 20 minutes1

Check Contacts behave like resistors, not diodes, on a test structure.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolTube furnace
PurposeMake good ohmic contacts
Temperature400 °C1
Time20 min1
ControlContact resistance on test structures
Out of controlContacts rectify: sinter longer; junctions short: the sinter ran too hot

Process engineer

Aluminium and silicon dissolve into each other a little at the interface, breaking through any thin oxide left in the hole3. Too much and the aluminium spikes down through a shallow junction, which is why later processes added barrier layers.

Then and now

From evaporated aluminium to copper damascene

Aluminium wired chips for almost forty years. Copper conducts better but cannot be etched easily, so it is laid into trenches instead.

  • Noyce’s patent, filed in 1959, ran aluminium leads over the oxide to the junctions5
  • Teaching run: evaporated aluminium measured 0.1229 Ω per square1
  • IBM announced full copper wiring in production CMOS in 19976
  • Bulk copper resists less than bulk aluminium: 1.68 against 2.65 µΩ·cm4

Institution-only The aluminium etch is a wet chemical step and is institution-only. Evaporators use high voltage and high vacuum and are run only by trained users.

What the models leave out

  • Step coverage over the contact edges is drawn as perfect; evaporation from a small source thins the film on steep walls.
  • Thin-film resistivity is taken as the bulk value, so the film thickness is a slight underestimate.
  • Junction spiking, hillocks and electromigration are mentioned but not modelled here.
  • No etch chemistry is given: it is institution-only.
