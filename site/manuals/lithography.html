MANUAL 4A · AREA 04 · 10 OPERATIONS

Print a pattern with light

Photolithography: prime, coat, bake, align, expose, develop, inspect

Every layer of a chip starts as a picture. Lithography copies that picture from a mask into a thin light-sensitive film called photoresist. The film then protects some parts of the wafer while the next step (etch or implant) changes the rest. A modern chip goes through this loop dozens of times.

The step list follows a public baseline traveler, the UC Berkeley Marvell Nanolab cmos150 run card1, and the run card of a real four-mask NMOS run in a university teaching cleanroom2.

OP 4A.01

Prime the surface

Expose the wafer to hexamethyldisilazane (HMDS) vapour. It turns the oxide surface water-repellent so the resist sticks.

  • HMDS prime, program 1 on the coat track1
  • teaching run: 10 min dehydration bake at 110 °C, then 5 min of primer vapour2

Check Water beads up on a test wafer instead of spreading.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolCoat track, vapour-prime module
PurposeAdhesion promotion before coat
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlContact angle on a monitor wafer
Out of controlResist lifts during develop: re-clean and re-prime

Process engineer

HMDS caps surface hydroxyl groups with methyl groups, so the surface stops attracting water. HMDS itself is a hazardous chemical and is handled only inside the closed prime oven.

2

OP 4A.02

Dispense the resist

Drop a few millilitres of liquid resist onto the centre of the wafer.

  • a few ml per wafer3

Check One round puddle, no bubbles.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolCoat track, spin-coat cup
PurposeDeliver resist
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlDispense volume; bubble-free supply
Out of controlComets and streaks: filter or degas the resist

Process engineer

Resist can be dispensed onto a still wafer (static) or onto a slowly turning one (dynamic). Air bubbles in the resist take about 20 minutes to escape from thin resists and hours from viscous ones3.

3

OP 4A.03

Spin it thin

Spin the wafer fast. The resist flings outward and thins to an even film as its solvent evaporates.

  • 2000–4000 rpm for 20–30 s3
  • Berkeley baseline: 4500 Å of UV-2101
  • teaching run: 3000 rpm for 30 s2

Check An even colour across the wafer. A thicker ring at the edge (the edge bead) is normal and is removed.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolCoat track, spin-coat cup
PurposeUniform resist film
Spin speed2000–4000 rpm3
Spin time20–30 s3
Ramp rate (smooth wafers)above 1000 rpm/s3
Thickness (example)4500 Å UV-2101
Teaching run3000 rpm, 30 s2
ControlFilm thickness by reflectometry; uniformity map
Out of controlToo thick: raise spin speed. Doubling the speed thins the film about 1.4×

Process engineer

t ∝ 1 / √ω

Thickness falls roughly as one over the square root of spin speed, so t ∝ 1/√ω and doubling the speed thins the film by about 1.4×3. Thinning stops once enough solvent has evaporated that the film no longer flows. After spinning, about 10 % (thin resists) to 35 % (thick resists) of the film is still solvent3.

4

OP 4A.04

Soft bake

Bake the wafer briefly on a hotplate to drive out most of the remaining solvent.

  • solvent falls to about 3–5 %3
  • teaching run: 90 °C for 90 s on a hotplate2

Check The film is dry and no longer tacky.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCoat track, hotplate
PurposeRemove solvent, fix film
Residual solvent after bakeabout 3–5 %3
Teaching run90 °C, 90 s2
ControlHotplate temperature log; thickness after bake
Out of controlUnder-baked film smears and dissolves too fast in develop

Process engineer

The soft bake is also called the post-apply bake (PAB)4. Too little bake leaves solvent that speeds dissolution everywhere; too much starts to destroy the light-sensitive compound.

5

OP 4A.05

Align the mask

Load the photomask, a glass plate with a chrome pattern, and line its marks up with the marks the last layer left on the wafer.

  • chrome mask, one per layer1
  • pre-alignment on1
  • teaching run, manual aligner: 0.2–1.4 µm overlay error2

Check The alignment marks overlap within the tool’s overlay limit.

Operation specification · Tier 0 · safe
ToolStepper or scanner, reticle stage
PurposeRegister this layer to the last
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlOverlay error on alignment marks
Out of controlOverlay out of limit: rework (strip resist and repeat from step 1)

Process engineer

Each layer must land on top of the last. The error between them is called overlay, and it is measured on dedicated marks after develop. A failed layer is reworked: the resist is stripped and the module runs again, because nothing has yet changed the wafer underneath.

6

OP 4A.06

Expose

Flash ultraviolet light through the mask. Where light lands, the resist changes chemistry and will later wash away.

  • dose 30 mJ/cm² in the Berkeley baseline1
  • smallest pitch λ / (2·NA)5
  • teaching run: i-line contact exposure, 9 s2

Check The tool reports the planned dose and focus for every field.

Operation specification · Tier 1 · care
ToolStepper or scanner (i-line, KrF, ArF, ArF immersion, EUV)
PurposeWrite the latent image
Dose (example)30 mJ/cm²1
Smallest pitch, one exposureλ / (2·NA)5
Teaching runi-line contact, 9 s2
ControlDose and focus per field; focus-exposure matrix on monitors
Out of controlLines too wide or narrow: adjust dose. Lines fade at the wafer edge: check focus and flatness

Process engineer

pitch_min = λ / (2·NA)

The lens can only collect so many of the diffraction orders that a fine pattern sends out. With tilted (off-axis) light the smallest printable pitch is λ / (2·NA), which is k₁ = 0.25 in the Rayleigh form5. The Physics Lab’s aerial-image simulator computes this image live.

OP 4A.07

Post-exposure bake

Bake again. In chemically amplified resist the light made a little acid, and the heat lets each acid molecule change many more.

  • “chemical amplification”4
  • post-exposure bake, program 11

Check Bake time and temperature match the resist’s sheet.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCoat track, hotplate
PurposeAmplify the latent image
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlHotplate uniformity; delay between expose and bake
Out of controlLine width drifts with bake temperature: requalify the hotplate

Process engineer

One photon makes one acid molecule. During the post-exposure bake (PEB) that acid acts as a catalyst and changes many polymer groups, so the image is ‘amplified’4. Acid also diffuses during the bake, which blurs the edge a little: one source of line-edge roughness.

8

OP 4A.08

Develop

Flood the wafer with developer. The exposed resist dissolves and the oxide shows through the openings.

  • exposed areas dissolve quickly4
  • aqueous alkaline developer: institution-only handling
  • teaching run: 60 s2

Check Openings are clear down to the oxide, with no scum.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolCoat track, develop module
PurposeTurn the latent image into openings
Develop time, teaching run60 s2
ControlAfter-develop inspection; CD on test structures
Out of controlScum in openings: descum in a short oxygen plasma, or rework

Process engineer

In a positive resist the exposed regions become very soluble and are removed quickly4. Developer chemistry is handled inside the track by trained staff; this manual describes it only conceptually.

9

OP 4A.09

Inspect and measure

Look at the pattern under a microscope, then measure line width and overlay.

  • CD-SEM (critical-dimension scanning electron microscope)
  • overlay metrology

Check Critical dimension (CD) and overlay are inside their limits. If not, strip and redo the module.

Operation specification · Tier 0 · safe
ToolOptical microscope, CD-SEM, overlay tool
PurposeDecide pass, rework or scrap
Set by the tool program; no public number to cite.
ControlCD and overlay against limits
Out of controlRework: strip and repeat from step 1

Process engineer

This is the last moment a mistake is cheap. After etch or implant the wafer itself has changed and cannot be reworked.

OP 4A.10

Hard bake

Bake the patterned resist in an oven so it is tough enough to survive the etch or implant that comes next.

  • more than 30 min at 120 °C in the oven1

Check Resist edges stay sharp, not rounded.

Operation specification · Tier 2 · heat or equipment
ToolConvection oven
PurposeHarden resist for etch or implant
Hard bake> 30 min at 120 °C1
ControlOven temperature log
Out of controlResist flows and rounds: bake was too hot

Process engineer

The Berkeley run card also lists a descum step here, skipped when no tool for the wafer size was available1. Next the wafer goes to Manual 5, Etch, where the resist protects the oxide under it.

Variant

Variant: extreme ultraviolet (EUV) at 2 nm-class nodes

EUV light is absorbed by glass and even by air, so an EUV scanner works in vacuum and uses mirrors instead of lenses. The mask is a mirror too.

  • Wavelength 13.5 nm6
  • Light is made when a CO₂ laser hits a falling tin droplet with two pulses, up to 50,000 times a second6
  • Multilayer mirrors, typically 40 Mo/Si bilayers, reflect up to 70 %7
  • The optics shrink the mask pattern 4× onto the wafer6
  • NXE: NA 0.33, 13 nm resolution. EXE (High-NA): NA 0.55, 8 nm resolution6
  • So few photons land per feature that random ‘stochastic’ defects appear: missing or bridged lines8

Institution-only The tin plasma source uses a high-power industrial laser inside a sealed machine. It is described here only to explain how the tool works.

What the models leave out

  • Real recipes depend on the exact resist, film stack and tool. The ranges here are teaching ranges from public sources, not a qualified recipe.
  • Reflections from the layers under the resist (standing waves) and anti-reflective coatings are not drawn.
  • Optical proximity correction (OPC), source-mask optimisation and multiple patterning are covered in the lithography chapter, not in this module.
  • Developer and primer chemistry are named but never given as concentrations or procedures.
