Safety tiers

The same tiers as the process manuals. Tiers 0 and 1 are for home or a classroom; tier 2 belongs in a supervised teaching lab; tier 3 is institution-only and shown here without any recipe.

  • Tier 0 · anywherePaper, a pencil, kitchen things or a spreadsheet. Nothing hot, sharp or powered beyond a multimeter’s own battery.
  • Tier 1 · home or classroom, with careLow-voltage electronics from batteries or a USB supply, a labelled Class 2 laser pointer, warm water or sunlight. An adult should help with anything hot.
  • Tier 2 · teaching labEquipment that belongs in a supervised teaching lab, such as a four-point probe station. Done there, under that lab’s rules.
  • Tier 3 · institution onlyHazardous chemistry, furnaces or gases. Explained here conceptually only: no recipes, concentrations or temperatures are published.

EXPERIMENT 01Tier 030 minutes

Sprinkle sand on a wafer and count the good dies

If dust lands at random, what fraction of chips escapes it, and how does that depend on chip size?

How it maps to chip making

A particle that lands on a die usually kills it. Each grain you drop is a particle; each square of the grid is a die.

  • Cleaning theatre →
  • Yield and cost lab →
  • Manual 2: cleaning →

You need

  • A large circle drawn on paper as the wafer
  • Grids of squares of four sizes drawn inside four copies of the circle
  • A small handful of rice, lentils or dry sand
  • A ruler

Method

  1. Draw four identical circles. Fill each with a grid of squares of one size, for example 1, 2, 3 and 4 cm across.
  2. Count the whole squares inside each circle. Ignore partial squares at the edge, as fabs do.
  3. Hold the same number of grains well above each circle and let them fall. Spread them gently if they clump.
  4. Count the squares with no grain in them. Yield is that count divided by the whole squares.
  5. Write one row per grid: the area of one square in cm², and the yield as a fraction.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Die area (cm²) · Yield (fraction). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with D = 0.08 per cm², an example value.

Defect density D
—
R² of the line
—

Add at least three readings to fit a line.

ln Y = −D·A

Compare with

  • With random defects, yield falls as Y = exp(−D·A): double the die area and you square the yield. This is the Poisson yield model.
    murphy1964
  • Real defects cluster, so fabs use the negative-binomial model, which loses fewer large dies than Poisson predicts.
    stapper1973stapper1983

What the model leaves out

  • Grains bounce and clump; real particles stick where they land.
  • Not every particle kills a die: only those larger than a critical size, in a sensitive spot.
  • Clustering, which the negative-binomial model adds.

EXPERIMENT 02Tier 020 minutes

Measure Moore’s law yourself

How many years does it take for the number of transistors on a chip to double?

How it maps to chip making

Every chip in the site’s chip list is a data point. Taking log₂ of the count turns exponential growth into a straight line.

  • Moore’s law chart →
  • Chips →

You need

  • Nothing: the real data is built in
  • A spreadsheet if you want to add chips of your own

Method

  1. Press “Use real data” to load every production chip from the site’s chip list: year and transistor count.
  2. The fit plots log₂(count) against year. The slope is doublings per year.
  3. Delete the oldest or newest rows and refit. Does the doubling time change with the era you choose?

Fit your data

One row per reading: Year · Transistors (count). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Production chips from data/chips.json (the same set as the Moore’s law chart).

Doubling time

log₂ N = a + year / T

Compare with

  • Moore first projected a doubling every year (1965), then revised it to every two years (1975).

  • The list favours famous chips, not a random sample.
  • Transistor count says nothing about cost per transistor, which is what Moore’s argument was about.

EXPERIMENT 03Tier 11 day

Watch dye diffuse into gelatin

Dopants spread into silicon by diffusion. Does the front move steadily, or does it slow down?

How it maps to chip making

Gelatin plays the silicon and food colouring plays the dopant. A drop on the surface is a constant surface source, like a predeposition.

  • Diffusion theatre →
  • Manual 6A: diffusion →

You need

  • Unflavoured gelatin set in a clear straight-sided glass (an adult makes it with hot water)
  • Food colouring
  • A ruler with millimetres
  • A clock

Method

  1. Let the gelatin set fully and come to room temperature.
  2. Cover the top with a thin layer of coloured water. Start the clock.
  3. At growing intervals, measure how far below the surface the colour reaches. Use the same judgement of “coloured” each time.
  4. Write one row per reading: time in minutes and depth in millimetres.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Time (min) · Depth (mm). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with a slope of 0.06 mm²/min, an example value.

Slope of depth² against time
—
Time offset
—
R² of the line
—

Add at least three readings to fit a line.

x² = k (t − t₀)

Compare with

  • From a constant surface source, any fixed concentration sits at a depth x = 2√(Dt)·erfc⁻¹(C/Cs). Depth squared therefore grows in proportion to time, just as for dopants in silicon.
    plummer2000

What the model leaves out

  • The slope is 4D times a factor set by where you judge the colour ends, so it gives D only up to that factor.
  • Gelatin swells and warms; silicon’s diffusivity depends steeply on temperature instead.

EXPERIMENT 04Tier 045 minutes

Build a capacitor from foil and paper

How does a capacitor depend on its area and on the thickness of the insulator between the plates?

How it maps to chip making

The gate of every MOSFET is a capacitor: metal, a thin insulator, then silicon. This one is foil, paper, foil.

  • MOS capacitor lab →
  • Oxidation theatre →

You need

  • Two sheets of kitchen foil cut to the same size
  • Plain paper
  • A heavy book to press the stack flat
  • A multimeter with a capacitance range

Method

  1. Measure one paper sheet’s thickness: measure a stack of many sheets and divide.
  2. Put one paper sheet between the two foils, press under the book and read the capacitance. Keep the leads still.
  3. Repeat with two, three and four sheets, then with smaller foils.
  4. Write one row per reading: foil overlap area in cm², paper thickness in µm and capacitance in pF.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Area (cm²) · Thickness (µm) · Capacitance (pF). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with 100 cm² of foil, 100 µm sheets and εr = 2.5, all example values.

Relative permittivity of the paper
—
R² of the line
—

Add at least three readings to fit a line.

C = εr ε₀ · A/t

Compare with

  • C = εr ε₀ A / t. Silicon dioxide has εr = 3.9, which is why high-k insulators replaced it once gate oxides grew too thin.
    sze2006robertson2006
  • Real measurements stray: the 2024 cleanroom run’s oxide capacitors read up to about four times away from this ideal, because of the meter’s series resistance.
    cleanroom_run_char2024

What the model leaves out

  • Air gaps between foil and paper add a thin, low-permittivity layer in series.
  • Fringing fields at the edges add capacitance that the formula ignores.

EXPERIMENT 05Tier 130 minutes

Read a CD’s track pitch with a laser pointer

Can you measure a spacing of about a micrometre with a ruler and light?

How it maps to chip making

A disc’s tracks are a diffraction grating, just like the lines on a photomask. A lens prints a pattern only if it catches the diffracted orders.

  • Lithography theatre →
  • Lithography optics lab →
  • Manual 4A: lithography →

You need

  • A labelled Class 2 laser pointer with its wavelength printed on it
  • A CD or DVD
  • A wall or screen and a tape measure

Method

  1. Never look into the beam or point it at anyone. Keep reflections on the wall.
  2. Shine the pointer through a clear spot on the disc, or reflect it straight back off the data side, onto a wall a metre or so away.
  3. Measure the distance L from disc to wall.
  4. Measure how far each bright spot sits from the central one: order m = 1, 2 and so on, on both sides.
  5. Type L and the wavelength into the boxes, then one row per spot: the order m and its distance in millimetres.

SafetyUse only a laser labelled Class 2 (or Class 1). Never look into the beam, and keep reflections away from eyes.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Order m · Distance from centre (mm). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with the CD pitch, 650 nm and L = 1 m; the wavelength and distance are example values.

Track pitch d
—
R² of the line
—

Add at least three readings to fit a line.

sin θ = m λ / d

Compare with

  • The CD standard sets the track pitch at 1.6 µm.
    ecma130
  • The DVD standard sets it at 0.74 µm, so a red laser shows only the first order.
    ecma267
  • Class 2 is limited to 1 mW of visible light; never use an unlabelled or higher-class laser.
    iec60825

What the model leaves out

  • The grating equation d sin θ = m λ assumes the beam hits the disc square on.
  • A photomask is imaged by a lens, which collects only some orders; here the wall sees them all.

EXPERIMENT 06Tier 145 minutes

Measure a diode’s current–voltage curve

How does the current through a silicon diode grow with voltage?

How it maps to chip making

Every transistor contains p–n junctions. This is the same junction, in a glass package you can hold.

  • p–n junction lab →
  • Diffusion theatre →

You need

  • A small silicon signal diode
  • Two AA cells in a holder
  • A potentiometer and a resistor of about a kilohm in series
  • Two multimeters (one for volts, one for milliamps)

Method

  1. Wire cells, potentiometer, resistor and diode in a loop, with the diode’s band towards the negative side.
  2. Put one meter across the diode (volts) and the other in the loop (milliamps).
  3. Turn the potentiometer in small steps and note both readings each time, from a few microamps up to a few milliamps.
  4. Write one row per step: diode voltage in volts and current in milliamps. Note the room temperature.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Voltage (V) · Current (mA). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with Is = 3 nA and n = 1.9, example values.

Ideality factor n
—
Saturation current Is
—
R² of the line
—

Add at least three readings to fit a line.

ln I = ln Is + V / n VT

Compare with

  • Shockley’s junction law is I = Is [exp(V / n VT) − 1] with VT = kT/q. An ideal diffusion current gives n = 1; recombination in the depletion region pushes n towards 2.
    shockley1949sze2006
  • The 2024 cleanroom run’s own p–n diode turned on at about 0.44 V.
    cleanroom_run_char2024

What the model leaves out

  • At high current the diode’s series resistance bends the line down.
  • The diode warms as current rises, which shifts the curve.

EXPERIMENT 07Tier 145 minutes

Estimate Planck’s constant from LED colours

Why does a blue LED need more voltage than a red one?

How it maps to chip making

An LED is a p–n junction in a direct-gap semiconductor. Each electron that crosses the gap gives one photon, so the gap sets the colour.

  • Band gaps lab →
  • Device Atlas →

You need

  • LEDs of four or more colours, with their peak wavelengths from the seller
  • The same circuit as the diode experiment
  • Two multimeters

Method

  1. Wire each LED in turn in the diode circuit.
  2. Raise the voltage slowly until the current reaches the same small value for every LED. Note the voltage: that is its turn-on voltage.
  3. Write one row per LED: peak wavelength in nanometres and turn-on voltage in volts.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Wavelength (nm) · Turn-on voltage (V). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with h = 6.0 × 10⁻³⁴ J s and a 0.1 V offset, example values.

Compare with

  • The exact value is h = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J s.
    codata2018
  • Band gaps of LED materials are tabulated for each alloy.

EXPERIMENT 08Tier 145 minutes

Find a MOSFET’s threshold voltage

At what gate voltage does a transistor turn on, and how fast does its current grow after that?

How it maps to chip making

A small discrete n-channel MOSFET is the same device as one transistor on a chip, only much larger.

  • MOSFET lab →
  • Manual 9: device modules →

You need

  • A small n-channel enhancement MOSFET for logic-level use
  • A 5 V USB supply or a battery pack
  • A potentiometer and a resistor of a few hundred ohms
  • Two multimeters

Method

  1. Tie the gate to the drain. Then the transistor is always in saturation, so one sweep gives the square law.
  2. Feed the drain through the resistor and the potentiometer, with the source to negative.
  3. Step the potentiometer and note the gate voltage and drain current at each step.
  4. Write one row per step: gate voltage in volts and drain current in milliamps.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Gate voltage (V) · Drain current (mA). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with Vt = 1.5 V and K = 40 mA/V², example values.

Threshold voltage Vt
—
Gain factor K
—
R² of the line
—

Add at least three readings to fit a line.

√ID = √K (VGS − Vt)

Compare with

  • In saturation a long-channel MOSFET follows ID = K (VGS − Vt)², so √ID is a straight line that crosses zero at Vt.
    sze2006schroder2006
  • The 2024 cleanroom run’s test transistors measured Vt from −3.0 to +7.5 V, most between −1.8 and 0 V.
    cleanroom_run_char2024

What the model leaves out

  • Below threshold the current is exponential, not zero; the fit uses only the upper points.
  • Power transistors heat up, and mobility falls as the gate field rises.

EXPERIMENT 09Tier 040 minutes

Draw pencil resistors and separate the contacts

When you measure a resistor, how much of the resistance is the contacts and how much is the film?

How it maps to chip making

Fabs measure contact resistance with test structures of the same width and different lengths. A graphite strip on paper is the film.

  • Probe theatre →
  • Manual 11: probe →

You need

  • A soft pencil (6B or softer)
  • Paper and a ruler
  • A multimeter on its resistance range
  • Two paper clips or crocodile clips

Method

  1. Draw one strip of even width, heavily shaded, about 15 cm long.
  2. Clip the meter’s leads at a fixed start and at points further along.
  3. Read the resistance at several lengths, keeping the clip pressure the same.
  4. Type the strip width, then one row per reading: length in millimetres and resistance in ohms.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Length (mm) · Resistance (Ω). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

  • R = 2Rc + Rs L/W. The slope gives the film; the intercept at L = 0 gives both contacts.
    berger1972schroder2006

EXPERIMENT 10Tier 11 hour

Turn a diode into a thermometer

At a fixed current, how does a diode’s voltage change with temperature?

How it maps to chip making

Chips sense hot spots with on-die diodes. The physics is the saturation current, which rises steeply with temperature.

  • Carriers and band gaps lab →
  • Self-heating lab →

You need

  • The diode circuit from experiment 6
  • A cup of warm water and a kitchen thermometer
  • A small plastic bag to keep the diode dry

Method

  1. Set a small, fixed current and leave the potentiometer alone from now on.
  2. Put the bagged diode in cool water beside the thermometer. Wait a minute and note temperature and voltage.
  3. Add warm water in steps (never hotter than a comfortable bath) and note both again each time.
  4. Write one row per reading: temperature in °C and diode voltage in volts.

SafetyUse warm tap water only. Keep mains-powered equipment away from the water.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Temperature (°C) · Diode voltage (V). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

EXPERIMENT 11Tier 12 hours

Print a pattern with sunlight

How small a line can a mask print when it does not touch the paper?

How it maps to chip making

Sun-print paper is a photoresist that water develops. Early chip makers printed masks in contact or close proximity, just like this.

  • Lithography theatre →
  • Manual 4B: photomask →

You need

  • Sun-print paper sold for children’s crafts
  • A transparency sheet printed with sets of lines of falling width
  • Thin card or paper spacers
  • A tray of water

Method

  1. Lay a mask straight on the paper (no gap) and others on stacks of spacers of known thickness.
  2. Expose in sunshine for the time on the packet, then rinse in water.
  3. Find the narrowest line set that still printed as separate lines for each gap.
  4. Write one row per gap: gap in µm and smallest resolved width in µm.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Gap (µm) · Smallest line (µm). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

EXPERIMENT 12Tier 11 to 2 weeks

Grow a crystal from a seed

If a seed crystal sits in a saturated solution, how steadily does it grow?

How it maps to chip making

A silicon ingot grows from a small seed that sets the orientation of the whole crystal. Table salt grows from a seed in the same way, much more slowly.

  • Czochralski theatre →
  • Manual 1: wafer →

You need

  • Table salt and warm water (an adult pours)
  • A clean jar and a thread
  • A kitchen scale reading to 0.1 g

Method

  1. Dissolve salt in warm water until no more will dissolve. Let it cool and pour off the clear liquid.
  2. Tie a small, clear seed crystal to the thread and hang it in the liquid.
  3. Each day lift it out, dry it gently, weigh it and hang it back.
  4. Write one row per day: day number and mass in grams.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Day · Mass (g). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

m = m₀ + rate · day

Compare with

  • A Czochralski puller sets growth by pull rate and temperature gradient, so the ingot grows at a steady, chosen speed.

  • Extra crystals grow on the thread and the jar and steal salt from the seed.

How do you measure a film’s resistance when every contact adds resistance of its own?

Four needles in a line: current through the outer two, voltage across the inner two. The voltage needles carry no current, so their contacts drop no voltage.

  • A four-point probe station in a supervised teaching lab

Method

  1. Follow the lab’s own procedure and supervision for the probe station.
  2. Measure the voltage at several currents, in both directions.
  3. Write one row per reading: current in milliamps and voltage in millivolts.

EXPERIMENT 14Tier 3institution only

Read an etch rate from a test series

How do engineers turn a few timed test etches into a rate they can trust?

How it maps to chip making

Before etching real wafers, a lab etches test pieces for different times, measures what was removed and fits a line.

  • Etch theatre →
  • Manual 5: etch →

You need

  • Only in an institution’s cleanroom, under its training and rules. No chemistry is described here.

Method

  1. Trained staff etch test pieces for a series of times and measure the film removed.
  2. Plot removed thickness against time. The slope is the etch rate; the intercept on the time axis is the delay before etching starts.
  3. Repeat for the mask material: the ratio of the two rates is the selectivity.

SafetyInstitution only. Hazardous chemistry is never done at home; this page explains the method, not a recipe.

Fit your data

One row per reading: Time (s) · Removed (nm). Paste from a spreadsheet, type, or open a CSV file.

Made with 1 nm/s and a 5 s delay, example values.

Start delay

removed = rate · (t − t₀)

Compare with

  • A straight line with a short delay is what a steady, reaction-limited etch looks like.

  • Rates change with pattern density and loading, so a blanket test piece is only a start.
  • No chemistry, concentration or temperature is given: those belong to the institution’s own procedure.

How the fits work

Every experiment turns its readings into a straight line and fits it by ordinary least squares. The slope and intercept give the physics; their standard errors come from the scatter of the points about the line and are carried through to each result. “Load model data” fills the table with numbers made from the model itself, with a little noise added, and labels them as such: they are a way to try the fitter, not a measurement. The same code runs in Python (sim/transistor_sim/physics/fitting.py) and is checked against this page’s JavaScript by the tests.
